# Infection Control Protocol

Document ID: POL-INF-007
Effective Date: 2025-03-01
Department: Infection Prevention & Control (IPC)

## 1. Purpose

This protocol establishes standard and transmission-based precautions
to prevent the spread of healthcare-associated infections (HAIs)
among patients, staff, and visitors.

## 2. Standard Precautions

Standard precautions apply to all patients regardless of diagnosis and
include: hand hygiene before and after every patient contact, use of
gloves for contact with blood or body fluids, safe injection
practices, and respiratory hygiene/cough etiquette signage at every
entrance.

## 3. Hand Hygiene

Staff must perform hand hygiene using alcohol-based hand rub (or soap
and water if hands are visibly soiled) at the "5 moments": before
patient contact, before an aseptic procedure, after exposure to body
fluid, after patient contact, and after contact with the patient's
surroundings. Hand hygiene compliance is audited monthly by unit.

## 4. Transmission-Based Precautions

Three additional precaution categories apply based on the
suspected or confirmed pathogen: Contact Precautions (gown and
gloves; e.g. MRSA, C. difficile), Droplet Precautions (surgical mask
within 3 feet of the patient; e.g. influenza, pertussis), and Airborne
Precautions (N95 respirator and negative-pressure isolation room;
e.g. tuberculosis, measles, COVID-19 during high-transmission
periods).

## 5. Isolation Room Signage and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Every isolation room must display color-coded signage at the door
indicating the precaution category and required PPE. PPE carts must
be stocked immediately outside the room, and staff must don PPE before
entry and remove it before exiting, disposing of used PPE in the
designated bin inside the anteroom.

## 6. Environmental Cleaning

Isolation rooms require terminal cleaning with an EPA-registered
disinfectant effective against the relevant pathogen upon patient
discharge or transfer, before the room may be used for another
patient. High-touch surfaces (bed rails, call buttons, doorknobs) are
cleaned at minimum twice daily in all occupied rooms.

## 7. Outbreak Response

If two or more patients on the same unit develop the same
healthcare-associated infection within a 7-day period, the unit
charge nurse must notify Infection Prevention & Control immediately.
IPC will conduct an outbreak investigation, may recommend cohorting
affected patients, and may recommend temporary restriction of
elective admissions to the affected unit until the outbreak is
controlled.

## 8. Staff Health

Staff with a fever, active respiratory illness, or open/draining
wounds should not report to direct patient care duties and must
notify Occupational Health before returning to work.

## 9. Visitor Coordination

Infection control precautions for a given patient take precedence
over the general Visitor Policy; the unit charge nurse will inform
visitors of any required PPE before they enter an isolation room.
