# Emergency Discharge Procedure

Document ID: POL-DIS-014
Effective Date: 2024-06-15
Department: Case Management & Nursing Administration

## 1. Purpose

This procedure governs the discharge of patients under emergency or
urgent circumstances, including disaster evacuation, patient-initiated
emergency discharge, and rapid discharge to free capacity during a
surge event.

## 2. Categories of Emergency Discharge

There are three recognized categories: (a) medically-cleared rapid
discharge during a bed-capacity surge, (b) discharge against medical
advice (AMA) at the patient's insistence, and (c) evacuation discharge
during a declared facility emergency such as fire, flood, or power
failure.

## 3. Medically-Cleared Rapid Discharge

When Administration declares a capacity surge, the attending physician
may expedite discharge for medically stable patients. The physician
must complete an abbreviated discharge summary within 2 hours of the
decision, and case management must confirm a safe discharge
destination (home, family, or transfer facility) before the patient
leaves the unit.

## 4. Discharge Against Medical Advice (AMA)

A patient who insists on leaving before the care team recommends
discharge must be informed of the medical risks by the attending
physician and must sign an AMA form. If the patient refuses to sign,
the refusal itself must be documented by two staff witnesses. Patients
discharged AMA are still provided a medication list, follow-up
instructions, and emergency return precautions.

## 5. Evacuation Discharge

During a declared facility emergency, the incident commander
(typically the on-duty nursing supervisor) directs unit charge nurses
to triage patients into three groups: (1) medically stable patients
suitable for immediate discharge with family/transport, (2) patients
requiring transfer to a partner facility, and (3) patients requiring
continued in-place critical care with emergency power/equipment.
Discharge paperwork may be abbreviated to a one-page emergency summary
during Category 1 evacuations, with full documentation completed
retrospectively within 24 hours once the emergency is resolved.

## 6. Required Discharge Documentation (Standard Minimum)

Regardless of category, every emergency discharge must include, at
minimum: current medication list, allergy list, primary diagnosis,
follow-up care instructions, and a 24-hour emergency contact number
for the discharging unit.

## 7. Transportation

Case management is responsible for arranging safe transportation for
any patient who cannot arrange their own, including patients
discharged AMA who lack a ride, unless the patient explicitly declines
assistance in writing.

## 8. Post-Discharge Follow-Up

For rapid and evacuation discharges, a follow-up phone call must be
placed by nursing staff within 48 hours to confirm the patient's
condition and address any questions about medication or care
instructions.
