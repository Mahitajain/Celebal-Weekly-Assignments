# Pre-Surgery Requirements and Documentation

Document ID: POL-SUR-022
Effective Date: 2024-11-01
Department: Surgical Services

## 1. Purpose

This document lists the documents, tests, and clearances required
before a patient may be scheduled for and undergo an elective or
urgent surgical procedure.

## 2. Required Documentation Checklist

Before surgery, the patient's chart must contain: a signed informed
consent form specific to the procedure, a completed pre-anesthesia
evaluation, a current history and physical (H&P) dated within 30 days
of surgery, an updated medication and allergy list, and a signed
Advance Directive acknowledgment (patients are asked whether they have
one; it is not mandatory to have one, only to be asked).

## 3. Required Laboratory and Diagnostic Testing

Standard pre-operative testing includes a complete blood count (CBC),
basic metabolic panel, and coagulation panel (PT/INR, PTT) for
patients over 50 or with a bleeding-risk history. An electrocardiogram
(ECG) is required for patients over 40 or with known cardiac history.
A chest X-ray is required only if clinically indicated by the
anesthesiologist. A pregnancy test is required for all patients of
childbearing potential undergoing procedures under general anesthesia,
performed within 24 hours of surgery.

## 4. Anesthesia Clearance

The anesthesiology team must complete a pre-anesthesia assessment
(ASA physical status classification) no more than 30 days before
surgery, with a same-day re-check on the morning of the procedure.
Patients with an ASA score of IV or higher require documented
cardiology or pulmonology clearance before final scheduling.

## 5. Medication Reconciliation

Patients on anticoagulants (e.g. warfarin, apixaban) must have a
documented bridging or hold plan from the prescribing physician.
Diabetic patients on insulin require a same-day glucose management
plan from the surgical team. All home medications must be reconciled
against the surgical order set by a pharmacist or nurse at pre-
admission testing.

## 6. Fasting (NPO) Instructions

Patients must be NPO (nothing by mouth) for solid food for 8 hours and
clear liquids for 2 hours prior to a procedure requiring general
anesthesia, unless otherwise directed in writing by the
anesthesiologist for a specific medical reason.

## 7. Consent and Patient Education

The surgeon (not a delegate) must personally discuss the procedure,
risks, benefits, and alternatives with the patient before the consent
form is signed. Patients must also receive written post-operative
instructions and a 24-hour surgical unit contact number at the
pre-admission visit.

## 8. Day-of-Surgery Checklist

On the day of surgery, nursing staff completes a final "time-out"
verification of patient identity, procedure, and surgical site
marking, cross-checked against the chart, before the patient enters
the operating room.
